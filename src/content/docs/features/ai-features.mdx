## Creating Tasks with AI

AI can create tasks from scratch or build on existing ideas. Use the Task Writer to transform notes into structured tasks, complete with descriptions, priorities, and assignees.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Task Writer" value="task-writer">

1. Open a task in your project.
2. Type `<code class="language-bash">/task</code>` to open the Task Writer.
3. Describe your task in natural language, or paste an unstructured note.
4. Add details like priority, labels, and assignees in the **Draft** section.

The AI refines your input, suggests an appropriate title, and prepares a description with Markdown. You can edit the draft before confirming.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Write with AI" value="write-ai">

Use `<code class="language-bash">/ai</code>` or the **Write with AI** button in the task composer to generate content:
- **Title** — auto-generates from your prompt and context.
- **Description** — creates rich, formatted content including lists, tables, and inline links.
- **Draft** — shows the full AI-generated text for review and editing.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label=" Voice Dictation" value="voice">

Click the purple dictation mic in the task composer to start speaking. The AI transcribes your words into text in real time. You can continue editing the transcribed text after the mic is released.

<Aside type="tip">
Voice dictation works on desktop and mobile. On mobile, the mic slides to the left for one-handed use.
</Aside>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Task Suggestions" value="suggestions">

When creating a task, AI suggests titles and descriptions based on your previous tasks, board context, and project tags. Accepting a suggestion populates the task form, giving you a scaffold to edit and refine.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Editing AI-Generated Content

AI can enhance or summarize your existing content. Use the **AI Editor** to:
- Fix spelling and grammar
- Rewrite for clarity or tone
- Apply Markdown formatting
- Summarize long descriptions or comment threads

To invoke AI Editing:

1. Select the text you want to edit.
2. Open the **AI Options** or use a shortcut to open the context menu.
3. Choose **Rewrite**, **Summarize**, or **Fix Spelling & Grammar**.

AI respects embedded blocks like estimated time blocks (canvas elements) and preserves them intact.

### Summarize Ticket Dialog

Use the **Summarize** action in the AI Options menu (three dots or Ctrl+K) to summarize the current task. AI generates a concise summary of the ticket description and comments, with a focused view on the task context. After submitting:

- The AI writes the summary as a new comment on the task.
- The original comment text is preserved — nothing is replaced or collapsed.
- You can continue interacting with the task in the same thread.

The summary restores successfully streaming responses without cancellation mid-flight.

## AI Chat

The AI Chat panel gives you an intelligent assistant integrated with your workspace:
- **Ask questions** — retrieve ticket details, project context, or board information.
- **Generate content** — create descriptions, tasks, or notes on-the-fly.
- **Draft replies** — compose comments for your assigned tasks.

<Aside type="tip">
Use Ctrl+K to open the prompt composer and access Shortcuts (e.g., "Copy in a friendly tone," "Summarize with a bulleted list").
</Aside>

### Chat Interface

The AI Chat panel displays your current session with:
- A clean title that never spills into the message area
- A wider panel floor for comfortable reading across device sizes
- Past-sessions dropdown with scrollable, scrollbar-hidden layout
- Docked scope picker (Current board, Pinned board, or All boards)

<Aside type="tip">
To minimize title overlap with messages, the interface now truncates very long session titles and reserves dedicated title-height space. The panel expands vertically to prevent text from being dangerously close to the header edges.
</Aside>

### Past Sessions List

The AI chat includes a **past-sessions dropdown** showing your recent conversations. This list can contain many sessions (e.g., hundreds), so native scrollbars would be intrusive. Instead, the dropdown uses CSS to hide native scrollbars while preserving scrolling functionality, providing a clean container view.

### Suggested Questions

AI generates concise, action-oriented suggested questions based on your current context (task, board, or whole workspace). Suggestions are:
- **Verb-first** with a clear intended action
- Max 7 words for brevity and quick reading
- Free of hedging phrases like "Should I" or "Can you"
- Contextually aware to match the work at hand

This makes it easier to jump straight into actionable prompts.

### AI Chat Scope

The docked scope picker lets you limit conversations to a specific board or explore all boards:
- **Current board** — defaults to the board you're actively viewing; follows navigation.
- **Pinned board** — locks conversation context; @mentions work and auto-swap is disabled.
- **All boards** — explores the full workspace.

<Aside type="tip">
A **with vector search** indicator shows when the system is searching across multiple boards using contextual relevance.
</Aside>

### Chat Features

**Shortcuts** — Access common prompts like "Summarize this task," "Rewrite this comment," and "Create a task from this." Shortcuts appear as a dropdown when the composer has focus or you use Ctrl+K.

**Copy to AI Chat** — Highlight any comment and select `<code class="language-bash">Ctrl+K</code>` (or use the three-dot menu) to copy it into the chat for rewriting, summarizing, or context expansion.

**Summarize focused comments via Ctrl+K** — After selecting comment text or using the three-dot menu, choose Summarize. This opens the AI chat with an AI-generated summary of the selected text and automatically sets the current task context for follow-on dialogue. You can keep chatting in the same thread.

**Fullscreen Mode** — Expand the chat to fill the screen by clicking the fullscreen icon in the top-right corner. The floating/PIP controls move to the three-dot menu.

**Pause & Resume** — Use the pause button in the composer to stop AI generation mid-stream and resume when you're ready.

### HTML Formatting

AI-chat comments and task descriptions are rendered as HTML with Markdown shortcuts supported where appropriate. This prevents stray Markdown markup from leaking into rendered content and ensures consistent display across view modes.

## Comment TL;DR Summaries

Comments with **120 or more words** automatically get a short AI summary, shown as a dimmed **TL;DR row above the comment**. The full comment always stays visible; nothing collapses.

- The row shows the first summary line clamped to one line. Click it to expand the full summary as bullets (a chevron appears on hover), exactly like the task summary at the top of a ticket.
- Summary length grows with the comment: roughly **one line per 120 words**, capped at 6 lines.
- Summaries generate a few minutes after a comment is saved or edited, using the same AI models configured for task summaries. Teams with AI disabled get no summaries and no AI spend.
- Activity events (like pull request notifications) and comments shorter than the threshold are skipped. Editing a comment below 120 words removes its summary.

## High-Risk PR Review Routing

When a GitHub Pull Request is opened for a ticket with High risk, the PR is automatically routed to Valentin Review instead of AI Review. Medium, Low, or unset risk levels continue to use the AI lane.

This behavior keeps high-risk merges under human sign-off while allowing AI to handle routine PRs.

<Aside type="tip">
Valentin Review is a dedicated human reviewer lane separate from AI triage, ensuring security-critical and logic-sensitive changes get thorough validation before merging.
</Aside>

## Generating Views

AI can now create filtered views on your boards. Ask the AI to "Create a view called Outdated that shows tickets older than 3 months" and it will generate a view with the appropriate filters.

<Aside type="tip">
The system uses existing saved views to apply filters. Use `<code class="language-bash">/views</code>` in task detail or context picker to see what views are available on a board.
</Aside>

## Skills

Skills let you cluster related tasks, projects, and workflows around shared expertise. AI agents can use skills to:
- **Find relevant tasks** — automatically select projects and boards where a skill applies.
- **Generate context-aware descriptions** — incorporate skill-specific terminology and patterns.
- **Suggest skill-adjacent actions** — propose follow-up tasks that align with team strengths.

To use AI with skills:
1. Create skills for key expertise areas (e.g., "Frontend," "Backend," "UX Research").
2. Assign team members to skills so AI knows who specializes in what.
3. Reference skills in AI prompts or descriptions. For example, "Summarize the frontend tickets on this board."

To use Strix:

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="From task detail" value="detail">

1. Open the task you want to scope for security review.
2. Press `<kbd>Ctrl+K</kbd>` and type **"Enable Pentesting"** to confirm the enabled agent.
3. Strix will cyclically review static checks on your exposed routes and surface findings as comments or tasks—no manual script changelog needed.

<Aside type="tip">
 Strix is available on every board with defined Scope; enable it via Ctrl+K once per project, then keep it on board-level defaults.
</Aside>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Automated recurring runs" value="scheduling">

1. Set a recurring schedule in your DevOps pipeline that invokes `<code class="language-bash">hypertask mcp pentest Strix</code>` or equivalent MCP endpoint.
2. Strix scans the configured scope and surfaces findings to your project's trackers (GitHub PR checks, Jira status changes, etc.).
3. Automate remediation flows—mark security tickets as blockers and route to your incident workflow.

<Aside type="tip">
 Runs respect existing privacy controls. You can add Strix to your board-level agents or create a dedicated "Security" agent board for PCI/DSS handling scenarios.
</Aside>

### Strix Integration with Boards & Views

You can explore Strix findings in real time using board filters and views. For example, open a "Security Findings" view and show items where **Strix_Note** contains "vulnerability" or configure "vulnerability/critical/high" tags on your board to group results by severity.

<Aside type="tip">
 Keep Strix on a board-level default schedule for weekly/fortnightly review patterns. Archive stale tickets as they close to keep findings current.
</Aside>

## FAQ

<details>
<summary>Can I use Strix on private apps or locally?</summary>
Strix focuses on publicly exposed routes; you can optionally include localhost or staging previews depending on your DevOps setup. For deeply private stacks, consider root-level Pentest tools or PyLink/PSI integrations.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I triage Strix findings?</summary>
Triage items as tasks on your project board. Tag them with "security" and assign remediation to your appropriate discipline. Archive false positives after manual verification.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Does Strix replace pentesters from an agency?</summary>
No. Strix is an automation layer and complement to human pentesters. Use it for low-risk surfaces and continuous monitoring; escalate suspicious findings to your agency or external pentesting firm.
</details>
